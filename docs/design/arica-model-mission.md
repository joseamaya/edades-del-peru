# La Defensa de Arica model mission

Design and status of `m9_guerra_pacifico`, the only mission of **La República**. It follows the
model-mission pattern ([`model-mission-pattern.md`](./model-mission-pattern.md)) and the campaign quality
bar ([`campaign-quality-bar.md`](./campaign-quality-bar.md)). The historical facts and their certainty live
in [`docs/historia/campanas/guerra_del_pacifico.md`](../historia/campanas/guerra_del_pacifico.md); this
document only references them.

## The historical problem

On 7 June 1880 the Chilean army stormed Arica. The Peruvian garrison under Francisco Bolognesi had refused
Salvo's surrender summons, mined the approaches with Teodoro Elmore's sappers and held the battery and the
cuartel through the bombardment. The plaza fell that day and Bolognesi died in the defence. The mission's
mechanic is that documented defence: refuse, prepare and hold, with the fall as the ending rather than a
counterfactual victory.

## Acts

| Act                      | Place                    | Player action                                              | Historical idea                                                             |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I · 5 June, the summons  | Port terrace and cuartel | Refuse the summons, mine the approaches, visit the battery | The political decision comes before the battle                              |
| II · 7 June, bombardment | Cuartel and fort         | Hold the cuartel, keep Bolognesi alive through the act     | The garrison endures artillery and the first assault waves                  |
| III · the assault        | Battery and last line    | Hold the last line for 120 s                               | The plaza falls; the epilogue tells Bolognesi's death and the _Manco Cápac_ |

Optional: visit the battery card. The mission has no counterfactual victory and no "Arica resists"
ending.

## Map

A 256 m transect generated by [`scripts/build_arica_heightmap.py`](../../scripts/build_arica_heightmap.py),
sampling the former 192 m composition 4/3 larger:

- **Pacific shore** (west): sea, beach and the coastal gravel.
- **Port terrace and cuartel** (centre-west): the player base, flat at 4 m.
- **Battery site** (centre-east): the authored `arica_site`, flat at 4 m, with the Morro outside the
  playable height range.
- **Northern approach**: the Chilean camp and the assault waves.
- **Eastern ridge**: low coastal hills frame the site.

Painted ground uses `ARICA_GROUND` (dirt terrace, sand shore, rock ridge) sampled at 128 × 128 over 256 m.
The worker crosses in ≈2:22 (`unitSpeedScale: 0.75`), inside the model-mission band.

## Site

`arica_site` (scale `0.8 × 256/192`, anchor `(179.7, 97.3)`, camera height 64) reuses the low-poly
`arica_battery` GLB, its heritage proxy and blocker, scaled with the terrace. The site has **no
construction phases**: it is an in-use port and fort, not a monument the player builds; this exception is
recorded here and in the quality bar. The Morro is not invented as a new mesh.

## Economy and units

- Workers, line infantry, riflemen, `REPUBLICA_DEFENSOR` and scouts; no artillery train, no
  cavalry (the 1880 garrison fought on foot in the scenario). The build set is the port and fort: no stable,
  artillery workshop or academy; the batteries belong to the fort and are not a trainable unit.
- The player base is the port terrace; the soldiers start around the cuartel and the fort.
- The royalist pressure is scripted: the bombardment wave, the sortie and the final assault.
  The enemy does not run an economy in this mission, and the definition says so: `enemy.economy: false`,
  `enemy.attacks: false` and `start.enemyBase: false` — the Chilean camp is the staging area of the waves,
  not a settlement with a centre, houses and a crew. The AI keeps its micro and its defence; the bombardment
  and the assault are authored waves, which is what the dossier describes as an abstraction.
- The garrison (Bolognesi included) starts `defensivo`: a line that chases a column off the objective stops
  holding it, and the stance returns it to the ground it was ordered to hold.
- The final assault is two columns (6 + 4), trimmed from 8 + 6. With no settlement the scripted enemy fights
  to the death instead of retreating to a base: at 8 + 6 the real-order pass reached the last line with 13
  defenders and never completed the 120 s hold; at 6 + 4 it completes it. `campaign-forces` pins the force
  contract (no enemy base, `defensivo` start, wave sizes).
- The bombardment and the assault are `hold` objectives on two named lines north of the port terrace
  (`ARICA_CUARTEL_LINE`, `ARICA_LAST_LINE`), not on the player's start: anchored on the terrace they
  completed after 60 s and 120 s with no order. Both are authored **world points**, not `ref: 'player'`:
  tied to the player's centre, a bombardment that razed it silently moved the objectives mid-act and the
  hold could not be finished.
- The waves enter from the northern approach (`ARICA_APPROACH_EAST`/`ARICA_APPROACH_WEST`) and attack those
  two lines, not the town behind them; the fort and the centre only fall if the line breaks. The 20 m
  scripted pursuit keeps a column pressing instead of freezing at the edge of its firing envelope.
- **No artillery on the attacking side.** The engine's siege piece (`ARTILLERY`) has 150 attack and a 56 m
  range against an 84 HP infantryman with a 13 m weapon: it removes the garrison from outside every player
  weapon, so a mission whose premise is "hold the line with the infantry you have" cannot field one and
  stay winnable. The bombardment is therefore **narration only** (intro, act card, dialogue): no gun is
  fielded and no off-board strike exists in the engine; the gun remains in the approved intro render, where
  it is a depiction and not a unit. `tests/campaign-forces.test.ts` guards both rules.

## Opening and closing

- Cinema intro (`introStyle: 'cinema'`, as the Tarapacá pilot): the port terrace with the fort and the
  Pacific (`cine-puerto`, push-in); a campaign map painted from this terrain where the sappers walk to the
  mines and the two Chilean columns draw themselves from `ARICA_APPROACH_EAST`/`ARICA_APPROACH_WEST` to
  `ARICA_CUARTEL_LINE`/`ARICA_LAST_LINE` (the same constants the waves use); the garrison on the cuartel
  line facing the first column (`cine-linea`, pan). It states the mission, not the fall: the act cards and
  the epilogue tell it. Regenerate the two 1920x1080 stills with
  `node scripts/capture_cinema.mjs --mission=m9_guerra_pacifico [--only=cine-linea]`. The older slide-intro
  captures were removed; [`scripts/capture_arica_intro.mjs`](../../scripts/capture_arica_intro.mjs) keeps only the
  epilogue.
- `MISSION_ART` (`m9_guerra_pacifico` → `media/generated/campaigns/republica/m9_guerra_pacifico/intro.webp`)
  and the briefing use the approved Blender render.
- Victory: the last line holds for the documented fall; the epilogue (`media/arica/intro-epilogo.webp`)
  tells the fall, Bolognesi's death and the scuttling of the _Manco Cápac_.
- Defeat: the fort falls before the final assault or Bolognesi dies during the bombardment.

## Tutorial

Five non-blocking steps: select the defenders, garrison the fort, learn the sappers' work, mine the
approaches and hold the last line. The summons is told by the intro and the dialogue, not by a quiz.

## Pacing

The pattern's target is 30–40 min. The real-command bot reaches the documented ending in about 4 min of
simulated time; a human playtest is still needed to measure the full path (the optional battery, the base
economy and the assault). The 30–40 target is **not** met and is documented as an exception instead of
padding the mission with invented acts. Re-measure after map, wave or economy changes.
