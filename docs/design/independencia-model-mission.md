# La Independencia model mission

Design and status of `m8_independencia`, the closing mission of La Independencia. It follows the
model-mission pattern ([`model-mission-pattern.md`](./model-mission-pattern.md)) and the campaign quality
bar ([`campaign-quality-bar.md`](./campaign-quality-bar.md)). The historical facts and their certainty live
in [`docs/historia/campanas/independencia.md`](../historia/campanas/independencia.md); this document only
references them.

## The historical problem

San Martín proclaimed independence in Lima in 1821. The final campaign was fought in the highlands: the
patriot cavalry held Junín on 6 August 1824, and Sucre's army resisted at Ayacucho on 9 December 1824 until
the royalist surrender, which sealed Peruvian independence. The mission's mechanic is the two-pampa arc:
hold Junín, march south, and hold Ayacucho while Sucre lives.

## Acts

| Act            | Place               | Player action                                                | Historical idea                                       |
| -------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| I · Junín      | Pampa de Junín      | Hold Junín 90 s with the patriot cavalry, Sucre protected    | The cavalry action opens the final campaign; no firearms |
| II · La marcha | Junín–Ayacucho road | March to Ayacucho with the army                              | The campaign moves south to the decisive field        |
| III · Ayacucho | Pampa de Ayacucho   | Hold Ayacucho 90 s until the royalist surrender; Sucre alive | The victory seals independence and ends Spanish power |

Optional: take the royalist centre if it resists. The epilogue narrates the end of Spanish power.

## Map

A 256 m transect generated by
[`scripts/build_independence_heightmap.py`](../../scripts/build_independence_heightmap.py), sampling the
former 192 m composition 4/3 larger:

- **Patriot camp and the Junín pampa** (west).
- **The campaign route** to the **Ayacucho pampa** and the royalist centre (east).
- **Andean ridges and the dry quebrada** frame the high pampas.

Painted ground uses `INDEPENDENCE_GROUND` (pampa grass, terrace dirt, quebrada sand, ridge rock) sampled at
128 × 128 over 256 m. The worker crosses in ≈2:22 (`unitSpeedScale: 0.75`), inside the model-mission band.
The horizontal composition scales 4/3 (start offsets, hold radii and wave centres included; each royalist
column now enters from its own side and marches to Junín or Ayacucho; Junín sits on its own pampa ~43 m east of the patriot camp and about 100 m from Ayacucho (the hold asks for three riders there, so no cavalryman starts inside it; the cavalry opens at Junín and the infantry/artillery reinforcement joins when the hold is completed),
so the march between the two fields is a real displacement). The heightfield
keeps the 192 m vertical amplitude, so slopes read gentler; the site GLB scales uniformly with the terrace.
Both are presentation trade-offs, not a survey.

## Site

`independence_site` (scale `0.65 × 256/192`, anchor `(179.7, 94.7)`, camera height 72) reuses the Ayacucho
chapel and patriot camp GLB, its heritage proxy, blocker and corridor, scaled with the terrace. The site has
**no construction phases**: the chapel and camp are in use, not a monument the player builds; the exception is
recorded here and in the quality bar.

## Economy and units

- Junín is cavalry-only, as documented: no riflemen, montoneras or artillery take part in the charge. The
  player roster is workers, cavalry and scouts; the patriot riflemen, montoneras and artillery are scripted
  reinforcements that join for the march and Ayacucho. The camp builds economy, a stable, a fortín and
  defences; no barracks, arsenal, artillery workshop or academy. Against royalist cavalry, dragoons and the
  Ayacucho garrison. This is 1824: firearms and horses are period-correct.
- Sucre rides with the cavalry as a protected named commander; the royalist centre is an optional
  destruction target, not the goal.
- The narrator is **Antonio José de Sucre**, a documented commander.

- The royalist army is a **scripted force**: `enemy.economy: false` and `start.enemyOpeningUnits: false`
  keep its centre and houses but stop the peasant economy, sawmill and mill. Its pressure is the Junín
  charge, the Ayacucho garrison (which mans the capture point) and the surrender hold; `attacks: false`
  leaves the offensive to those authored waves. Both waves are sized to their 90 s holds: with the scripted
  AI no part of the garrison marches on the camp, and the original counts made the final hold a wipe in the
  real-order playtest. The Junín charge is smaller too because its survivors can reinforce Ayacucho when the
  base is threatened; leaving it at seven kept the final hold unwinnable.

## Opening and closing

- Cinema intro (`introStyle: 'cinema'`, see `src/ui/CinemaIntro.ts`): the Ayacucho pampa and its chapel
  (`cine-ayacucho`, push-in); a campaign map painted from this terrain that resolves the mission's own points
  (`INDEPENDENCE_JUNIN`, `INDEPENDENCE_MARCH`, both wave entries) from the layout anchors: the cavalry to
  Junín, the royalist charge, the march to Ayacucho (dashed) and the Ayacucho column; the patriot cavalry on
  the Junín pampa facing the dragoons (`cine-junin`, pan). It does not tell the surrender. Stills:
  `node scripts/capture_cinema.mjs --mission=m8_independencia [--only=cine-junin]` (shots in
  `scripts/cinema-shots/independencia.mjs`); offline captures, so the triangle cap does not apply.
- The three act cards and the epilogue are captured in engine by
  [`scripts/capture_independencia_intro.mjs`](../../scripts/capture_independencia_intro.mjs): the Lima
  campaign camp, Junín, the march and Ayacucho, plus a dusk epilogue.
- `MISSION_ART` (`m8_independencia` → `media/independencia/intro-lima.webp`) and the briefing use the Lima
  capture; the Act II card uses the march capture.
- Victory: Ayacucho is held and Sucre lives. The epilogue (`media/independencia/intro-epilogo.webp`) narrates
  the royalist surrender and the end of Spanish power. Defeat: Sucre dies or the army is lost.

## Pacing

The real-command bot holds Junín, marches and holds Ayacucho in about eight minutes of simulated time (the
E2E runs it in seconds of wall-clock). A human playtest is still needed to measure the 20–30 min path; the
model pattern's 30–40 min band is not claimed.

## Balance

The royalist side is set to `normal` difficulty and aggression 1.0, and the Ayacucho hold is 90 s. These are
game-balance settings, not historical claims: with the 4/3 map and the scripted waves, the real-command
playtest could not keep the line alive for 120 s. The documented outcome (the royalist surrender) and the
units are unchanged.
