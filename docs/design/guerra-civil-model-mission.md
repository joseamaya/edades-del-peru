# La Guerra Civil model mission

Design and status of `m6b_guerra_civil`, the Inca civil war mission of La Senda del Sol. It follows the
model-mission pattern ([`model-mission-pattern.md`](./model-mission-pattern.md)) and the campaign quality
bar ([`campaign-quality-bar.md`](./campaign-quality-bar.md)). The historical facts and their certainty live
in [`docs/historia/campanas/guerra_civil_inca.md`](../historia/campanas/guerra_civil_inca.md); this document
only references them.

## The historical problem

After Huayna Cápac's death, Huáscar held Cusco and Atahualpa the north. The war culminated at Quipaipan
(1532), where Atahualpa's generals defeated Huáscar's army and captured him. Atahualpa won the civil war, but
the Spanish captured him at Cajamarca in November 1532. The mission's mechanic is the Quipaipan arc: march,
hold the pampa, and capture Huáscar without razing Cusco.

## Acts

| Act              | Place              | Player action                                                 | Historical idea                                             |
| ---------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| I · La marcha    | Quito–Cusco road   | Advance with Atahualpa and present battle at Quipaipan        | The dynastic war divides the Tahuantinsuyo                  |
| II · Quipaipan   | Pampa de Quipaipan | Hold the pampa 90 s with six soldiers                         | The battle breaks Huáscar's army                            |
| III · La captura | Huáscar's centre   | Move from the pampa and hold the capture 20 s with four units | The capture ends the war; the Cusco centre is not destroyed |

The Cusco centre stays standing: the victory is the capture, not the destruction of the city. The epilogue
narrates Cajamarca and the weakened empire.

The enemy base is labelled «Ejército de Huáscar», not «Cusco»: the capture is Huáscar's field headquarters at
Quipaipan, and the mission is not fought in the city. The Quipaipan garrison deploys from the pampa's edge instead of appearing on it, and the Act III relief
column enters on the Quito road,
behind the line, and marches on the camp — it used to appear beside the advance and still announce a rear
attack.

## Map

A 256 m transect generated by [`scripts/build_cusco_heightmap.py`](../../scripts/build_cusco_heightmap.py),
sampling the former 192 m composition 4/3 larger:

- **Quito camp** (west) and the road to Quipaipan.
- **Quipaipan** (east): the authored `cusco_inca_site` with the ushnu, the pampa and the Cusco centre.
- **Andean ridges and the dry quebrada** frame the basin.

Painted ground uses `CUSCO_GROUND` (pampa grass, terrace dirt, quebrada sand, ridge rock) sampled at
128 × 128 over 256 m. The worker crosses in ≈2:22 (`unitSpeedScale: 0.75`), inside the model-mission band.
The horizontal composition scales 4/3 (start offsets, radii and wave centres included). The heightfield
keeps the 192 m vertical amplitude, so slopes read gentler; the site GLB scales uniformly with the terrace.
Both are presentation trade-offs, not a survey.

## Site

`cusco_inca_site` (scale `0.7 × 256/192`, anchor `(166.4, 174.1)`, camera height 77.3) reuses the Cusco
ushnu and plaza GLB, its heritage proxy, blocker and corridor, scaled with the terrace. The site has **no
construction phases**: Cusco is a living capital and Quipaipan a battlefield, not a monument the player
builds; the exception is recorded here and in the quality bar.

## Economy and units

- Inca workers, warriors and honderos on both sides; `UnitKind.MUSKETEER` is the legacy ranged id and renders
  as a hondero in this era, not a firearm. No horses or firearms on the Inca side.
- Huáscar's headquarters is a **field command, not a town**: `enemy.economy: false` and
  `start.enemyOpeningUnits: false` keep its centre and houses but stop the peasant economy, and a scripted
  guard (four warriors and three honderos) mans the centre from the opening. The pressure is the Act II wave
  on the pampa and the relief column on the Quito road; the capture is not a walk-in.
- The narrator is **Atahualpa**, a documented protagonist; the enemy centre is Huáscar's headquarters at
  Quipaipan, not the city, and is not a destroy objective.

- Victory: the pampa is held and Huáscar is captured. The epilogue (`media/cusco-inca/intro-epilogo.webp`)
  narrates Cajamarca. Defeat: Atahualpa dies or the army is lost.

## Tutorial

The non-blocking steps cover selection, the march, the battle and the capture; the historical context stays
in the briefing, the chapter cards and the Codex.

## Tests and evidence

- Data: `tests/campaign.test.ts`, `tests/campaign-layout.test.ts`, `tests/authored-sites.test.ts`.
- Scale: `tests/campaign-scale.test.ts` enforces 256 m, 128² painted ground and the crossing band.
- E2E: `campaign-playtest.spec.ts` (real orders to the capture marker, no cheats),
  `campaign-completable.spec.ts`, `save-campaign.spec.ts` (mid-capture save/load) and `hud-layout.spec.ts`.
- Bench: `scripts/bench_scene.mjs` measures the opening (~703k tris / 436 calls) and the battle
  (~967k / 673); over the 600k target as m7/m9; `yarn bench:check`.
- Evidence: `yarn record --mission=m6b_guerra_civil --mode=clip` and the intro/epilogue captures.
- Save compatibility: `SAVE_VERSION` 11 rejects v10 m6b saves (their 192 m coordinates cannot be carried
  over); `REPLAY_VERSION` 38.

## Pacing

The real-command bot reaches the documented ending in about 2 min of simulated time in the E2E; a human
playtest is still needed to measure the 20–30 min path. The model pattern's 30–40 min band is not claimed.
