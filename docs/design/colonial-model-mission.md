# La Gran Rebelión model mission

Design and status of `m7_tupac_amaru`, the closing mission of **El Virreinato**. It follows the
model-mission pattern ([`model-mission-pattern.md`](./model-mission-pattern.md)) and the campaign quality
bar ([`campaign-quality-bar.md`](./campaign-quality-bar.md)). The historical facts and their certainty live
in [`docs/historia/campanas/tupac_amaru.md`](../historia/campanas/tupac_amaru.md); this document only
references them.

## The historical problem

In 1780 José Gabriel Condorcanqui, Túpac Amaru II, rose against the reparto, the mita and the obrajes.
Micaela Bastidas organised the supplies and the network of communities. The rebellion won at Sangarará, but
the January 1781 siege of Cusco failed when the relief from Lima broke it; Túpac Amaru was captured at
Langui on 6 April 1781, and he and Micaela were executed on 18 May 1781. The mission's mechanic is that arc:
muster, win the field, hold the siege, and withdraw when the history already decided the defeat.

## Acts

| Act                          | Place                    | Player action                                                    | Historical idea                                                     |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I · Tinta–Tungasuca (1780)   | Rebel valley camp        | Muster supplies with Micaela, raise a barracks and reach Tungasuca | The rebellion is a network of communities before it is an army      |
| II · Sangarará (18 Nov 1780)| Sangarará road and chapel| March, surround the royalist column and hold the field            | The great rebel victory that does not open Cusco                    |
| III · The Cusco siege (1781)| Slopes above Cusco       | Hold the siege lines; when the relief arrives, withdraw Túpac    | The siege fails; survival, not the city, is the objective           |

Optional: protect Micaela. Túpac's death is an explicit scripted defeat; the retreat requires him at the
departure point.

## Map

A 256 m transect generated by [`scripts/build_colonial_heightmap.py`](../../scripts/build_colonial_heightmap.py),
sampling the former 192 m composition 4/3 larger:

- **Valley and rebel camp** (north-west): the base, the barracks and Micaela's supply work turn.
- **Tungasuca** (south-west): the muster point that opens the march.
- **Sangarará** (centre): the road, the chapel (a typological landmark) and the royalist column.
- **Slopes above Cusco** (east): the siege lines; the authored `colonial_site` church is the city.
- **Camino a Langui** (departure): the withdrawal point.

Painted ground uses `COLONIAL_GROUND` (valley grass, terrace dirt, ridge rock) sampled at 128 × 128 over
256 m. The worker crosses in ≈2:22 (`unitSpeedScale: 0.75`), inside the model-mission band. The horizontal
composition scales 4/3; the vertical amplitude is kept from the 192 m authoring, so slopes read 25 % gentler
and `SITE_Y` stays at 5 m. That is a presentation trade-off, not a survey.

## Site

`colonial_site` (scale `0.65 × 256/192`, anchor `(179.7, 97.3)`, camera height 69.3) reuses the low-poly
Cusco church and plaza, its heritage proxy and blocker, scaled with the terrace. The site has **no
construction phases**: it is a city in use, not a monument the player builds; the exception is recorded
here and in the quality bar. The Sangarará chapel is a separate typological `colonial_church` decor.

## Economy and units

- Workers, militia, scouts and a missionary are the trainable roster; the few riflemen and the two horses
  in the opening are captured weapons and are not replaced. No rebel artillery train (not documented for
  1780). The player can build the town economy, a cuartel and a market.
- The royalist pressure is scripted (Sangarará, the Cusco sortie and the Lima relief); the city does not
  run an economy. The waves are labelled as an abstraction in the dossier.

## Opening and closing

- Cinema intro (`introStyle: 'cinema'`, as the Tarapacá pilot): the valley from Tinta toward Sangarará
  (`cine-valle`, push-in); a campaign map painted from this terrain where the rebellion marches from the
  Tinta camp to Tungasuca and on to Sangarará, the royalist column enters Sangarará from
  `TUPAC_SANGARARA_GARRISON` and the Cusco militias sally dashed from `TUPAC_CUSCO_SORTIE` onto the siege
  slopes (constants shared with the layout, objectives and waves); the rebel army mustered at the camp
  (`cine-rebelion`, pan). It states the mission, not its outcome: Sangarará, the relief and the retreat
  open as act cards, the capture and execution in the epilogue. Regenerate with
  `node scripts/capture_cinema.mjs --mission=m7_tupac_amaru [--only=cine-rebelion]`. The older
  `scripts/capture_colonial_intro.mjs` captures stay as briefing art and act cards.
- `MISSION_ART` (`m7_tupac_amaru` → `media/cusco/intro-valle.webp`) and the briefing use the valley capture in normal play; the draft `m7_colonial-intro` record was retired on 2026-11-16.
- Victory: the army and Túpac withdraw when the relief breaks the siege; the epilogue
  (`media/cusco/intro-epilogo.webp`) tells the capture at Langui and the execution of 18 May 1781.
- Defeat: Túpac dies, or the leadership is lost before the retreat.

## Tutorial

Six non-blocking steps: select the column, raise the barracks, complete the supply work turn, hold
Sangarará, hold the siege and withdraw Túpac. The historical context stays in the briefing, the chapter
cards and the Codex.

- Evidence: `yarn record --mission=m7_tupac_amaru --mode=clip` and the intro/epilogue captures.

  over); `REPLAY_VERSION` 34.

## Pacing

The agreed target for this pass is 20–30 min. The real-command bot reaches the documented ending in about
7 min of simulated time; a human playtest is still needed to measure the 20–30 min path. That is a
documented exception to the model pattern's 30–40 min band, not a claim that the band was reached; no
invented acts were added.
