# Terraza portuaria y batería de Arica (mission 9)

Scene design for `m9_guerra_pacifico`. The playable site is a project-owned
low-poly abstraction of Arica's port terrace, battery and warehouse. It is
**not** a survey and **not** a historical photograph.

## Sources

- `docs/historia/campanas/guerra_del_pacifico.md`
- `docs/historia/arquitectura.md`

## Runtime source

- GLB: `src/assets/sites/arica/arica_battery_lod0.glb` / `arica_battery_lod1.glb`.
- Source: `art-src/blender/mission-decor/arica_battery_lod0.blend` /
  `arica_battery_lod1.blend` (Git LFS).
- Build/export: `scripts/blender/build_mission_decor.py`.
- LOD1 is the RTS default; `?lod=0` previews LOD0.
- Scale `0.8 × 256/192`, site origin and anchor `(179.7, 97.3)` on the 256 m map.
- Authored heightfield: `src/model/defs/aricaHeightmap.ts`, generated by
  `scripts/build_arica_heightmap.py`. The 192 m composition is sampled 4/3 larger:
  the port terrace and player base are flat at 4 m; the Pacific shore and low
  coastal ridge frame the site. The painted ground weights (`ARICA_GROUND`) keep
  the terrace as dirt, the shore as sand and the ridge as rock.
- A 1 m pathfinding grid, monument blocker and a 10.7 m walkable corridor connect
  the base to the port.

## Mission acts

The mission follows the documented fall of the plaza (7 June 1880), not a counterfactual defence:

1. **Act I · 5 June — the summons.** Bolognesi and his chiefs refuse Salvo's surrender summons while the
   sappers mine the approaches; the battery card is an optional visit.
2. **Act II · 7 June, bombardment.** The garrison holds the cuartel and the fort; Bolognesi must survive
   this act.
3. **Act III · the assault.** The Chilean infantry storms the plaza; the player must hold the last line
   for the documented ending. Victory is the tactical fulfilment of the defence and the epilogue tells
   the fall, Bolognesi's death and the scuttling of the *Manco Cápac*.

The fort is the RTS abstraction of the port's battery/cuartel; the Morro itself stays outside the playable
height range, and only its map label sits on the coastal headland that closes the bay (it used to be placed
inland). The enemy camp on the northern approach and the assault waves are an abstraction of the
documented attack, not a literal order of battle.
